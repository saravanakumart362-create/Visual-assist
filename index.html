Navigating the Unseen

Visual Assist

A wearable aid for blind people. A camera recognises what is in the room, a LiDAR measures how far away it is, and a voice guides the wearer to what they ask for, around whatever stands in the way.

    What is said

      Questions and answers appear here.

      • Distances here are estimates from how large things look, so they begin with "about". The device measures them with its LiDAR.
      • Directions are given as the camera sees them. On a phone, use the back camera and point it the way you are facing.
      • Finding a way around obstacles needs the LiDAR sweep, so that part stays in the simulated room above. Face recognition is not included in the page.
      • Questions are asked in English, whatever language the answers are in.

      How it finds the way

      Each time you ask, the demo above goes through these four stages. They repeat after every instruction, so a turn that was too wide or a step that was too short is corrected as it happens.

      1. Look

        The head turns to five angles and takes a picture at each. A YOLOv5 model names what it sees: door, chair, table, person. In the demo this is the pale wedge that flashes in front of the user.

      2. Measure

        The head turns to the target and the LiDAR measures the distance. The reading is checked against how large the object looks in the picture, because a beam aimed at a door may be stopped by a chair in front of it.

      3. Plan

        The LiDAR sweeps 144 degrees in 6 degree steps. Each reading becomes an obstacle on a map with 10 cm cells, and the A* algorithm finds the shortest route that stays 35 cm clear of all of them. These are the red dots and the dashed line.

      4. Speak

        One action at a time: first a turn, and a walk only once the user faces a clear stretch. No walk is longer than what was just measured as clear, less a margin for uneven steps, and never more than five steps.

        What else it does

        Warns of obstacles
        "Bottle detected at 0.4 meters." The warning comes earlier the faster the wearer walks, and becomes "Stop" when a collision is less than a second and a half away.
        Adapts to the wearer
        It says more to someone who keeps pausing, and less on a trip made three times before. Walking speed is read from the LiDAR, since the device has no motion sensor.
        Recognises people
        Registered faces are announced by name, the most familiar first. A stranger can be registered by voice.
        Reads banknotes
        Indian notes from ₹10 to ₹2000, held up to the camera. A doubtful reading is offered as a guess, never stated as fact.
        Speaks four languages
        English, Malayalam, Hindi and Tamil, switched by saying "speak in Malayalam". Try the language menu in the demo.
        Answers questions
        "Where is the door?" "What is around me?" "How far?" "Who is this?" Commands are spoken; an earphone carries the replies.

        Measured so far

        These figures come from simulated rooms and from photos the models never saw during training. Nothing here has yet been measured on the assembled device.

        WhatResultHow it was measured
        Guidance 997 of 1,000 trips reached the door; the other three ended with the assistant saying it had stopped. Nobody was walked into anything. Random cluttered rooms in the simulator; half the simulated users turned and stepped imprecisely.
        Door detection 17 of 20 large indoor doors found. When it reports a door it is right about 7 times in 10. 229 held-out photos. Small and distant doors are often missed.
        Banknotes 390 of 400 notes named correctly and stated plainly; none stated wrongly. All 225 photos without a note were recognised as such. 625 held-out photos from the same collection as the training photos; not yet tried on a live camera.

        Run it yourself

        The same simulation runs in a terminal, and with a webcam the assistant answers typed questions about what it sees.

        pip install opencv-python ultralytics pyyaml pyserial gTTS

python main.py simulate               # this demo, in the terminal
python main.py --keyboard --show      # live on a webcam
python -m pytest                      # the tests
